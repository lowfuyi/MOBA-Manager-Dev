# Full-pipeline calibration (GDD section 9.1)

Real rosters, hero fit, hash-derived familiarity, the AI lineup rule and the AI draft, then the simulator (`tools/pipeline.py`). Phase 1 simplifications: Form 0, Morale 60, Fatigue 0, TeamMod fixed at the new-club Bond value (0.96%), MetaMod drawn per patch (20 patches of +-3%), familiarity from a v0 hash rule (3 signature heroes at 50 to 85, everything else 0 to 30). Seeds are fixed, so the numbers reproduce.

## Findings and decisions (P-003, 2026-11-07)

**Problem found with the v0 constants.** Drafting for familiarity beat drafting for composition by 3 to 6 points, and the default AI draft only tied the familiarity-heavy one. A drafter that maximises the rating points each pick adds ("expert" probe, no noise) beat the default by 0.0 points. So with v0 constants the draft rewarded comfort picks and nothing above them.

**Sweep** (each cell 800 side-swapped mirror matches, standard error about 1.8 points; first policy's win rate; `tools/pipeline_experiment.py`):

| Constants | default v fam | comp v fam | expert v fam | expert v default |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| v0: familiarity weight .10, synergy 2.5%, penalties 3/4%, cap 10%, lane counter 5% | 48.5% | 45.9% | 51.4% | 50.0% |
| lane counter 10% | 48.8% | 46.5% | 51.5% | 49.6% |
| lane 10% + familiarity .06 | 49.6% | 48.1% | 53.6% | 49.9% |
| **lane 10% + familiarity .06 + synergy 4%, penalties 5/6%, cap 15% (adopted)** | 50.2% | 49.1% | 54.8% | 51.0% |
| lane 15% + familiarity .05 + synergy 5%, penalties 6/7%, cap 20% | 51.2% | 50.8% | 55.9% | 52.0% |

**Decision:** the adopted row. It stops familiarity dominating (familiarity-heavy and composition-heavy drafting now tie), gives a careful drafter about 5 points over a comfort drafter, and keeps the cap rare (3.3% of all 32,768 lineups). The last row gains about one more point and was rejected because a 20% cap is a very large swing on a single screen. Changed: `HeroFactor` familiarity weight 0.10 to 0.06, synergy +2.5% to +4%, missing-need penalty 3% to 5%, damage-imbalance penalty 4% to 6%, CompMod cap 10% to 15%, lane counter 5% to 10%. The simulator itself is unchanged, so the golden vectors still hold.

**What the draft is worth, stated plainly.** The draft is a margin lever, not a game-winner. The typical gap between two sensible drafts is about 3 win-rate points, so it flips the winner in about 3% of matches; squad strength still dominates (+10 rating gap wins about 71%). A strong draft against a poor one is worth about 10 points. If playtests show the draft feels too small, the next step is widening MetaMod, not more CompMod.

**Mirror check.** The first 900-match runs gave Blue 48.2% and 47.9%, which looked like a failure. A 4,000-match check gave **50.8%** (SE 0.8) at side bonus 0.02, and 51.0% at 0.04, so the earlier numbers were sampling noise and the side bonus stays at 0.02. The mirror sample in this report is now 3,600 matches.

**Consistency.** CON barely changes a strong favourite's win rate against a weak club (see the table below): a favourite at +30 rating is already near the ceiling of the tanh contest. CON matters between close teams; the paired lever test for it is part of the remaining P-003 work.

**Game Plan (GDD 7.8, 9.2.1).** `tools/gp_qa.gd`: 6 matchups from 1 to 35 rating points apart, plans fitted on 800 training seeds and scored on 4,000 held-out seeds (standard error about 0.8 points per matchup). Gain is in win-rate points over the neutral plan.

| Quantity | Result | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Best single control, mean over matchups | Risk +0.87 | 1.0 |
| All other controls, mean | Aggression +0.29, Tempo +0.16, Fight style +0.12, Mid objectives +0.03, rest about 0 | 1.0 |
| Joint plan (all controls fitted together), mean | +1.56 | 3.0 |

| Coach suggestion, mean | +1.02 | n/a |

Reading: no plan dominates, and the best plan depends on the matchup. Risk is the strongest single lever because variance helps the underdog; its largest effect (+2.90 points) is for the 10% underdog, which is the intended comeback valve and stays far below the effect of squad strength (a 20-point rating gap is worth about 32 points of win rate, 82.5% in the calibration table). The coach gets about two thirds of the joint gain with no player effort. Zero-sum (9.2.9) and neutral-equals-plain-sim are asserted in `game/tests/game_plan_tests.gd` (1,547 checks pass). No coefficient change needed. Re-run after Team Bonds ships.

## Addendum (Alpha 0.2, 2026-11-08): training and fatigue schedules (GDD 7.3, 9.2 "no schedule is strictly dominant")

`tools/train_qa.gd` plays each of the 10 clubs through two league seasons under six manager policies (the human club only; every AI club trains by the coach's pick and rotates). Results do not feed back into training yet (no Form or Morale), so one run per club and policy is exact. "eff" is the mean over match days of the five starters' mid-game rating with each one's best hero at neutral meta, fatigue included, so growth, familiarity and fatigue all count. "base" is the plain Team Rating at the end of the season. Mean over the 10 clubs.

**First run, GDD base rate 0.5 points per training week.** Training lost: resting everybody beat the coach's training for 10 of 10 clubs in season 1 (eff 53.13 against 52.68) and for 9 of 10 in season 2, because a season of training added only +0.28 Team Rating but cost about 0.45 rating in fatigue. That is a dominant "never train" schedule, so the numbers were wrong. Fix (producer call, within the 7.3 formula): base rate 0.5 to 1.5 points per week and hero practice 1.5 to 3.0 familiarity per week. The formula, age factors, Coach and Facility terms and all fatigue numbers are unchanged.

**Second run, base 1.5, hero practice 3.0.**

| Policy | eff S1 | eff S2 | base S1 | base S2 | Wins S1 | Wins S2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| coach training, fixed lineup (the default) | 53.35 | 54.35 | 53.96 | 54.52 | 8.9 | 9.5 |
| coach training, rotate every match day | 53.47 | 54.51 | 53.82 | 54.39 | 8.9 | 9.1 |
| nobody trains | 53.53 | 53.84 | 53.18 | 53.16 | 9.0 | 9.2 |
| everybody practises a hero | 53.05 | 53.28 | 53.18 | 53.16 | 9.1 | 8.8 |
| starters rest, bench trains | 53.53 | 53.84 | 53.18 | 53.16 | 9.0 | 9.2 |
| train 9 match days, then rest | 53.85 | 54.54 | 53.61 | 53.95 | 9.0 | 9.5 |

Reading:
- **Training is an investment.** Resting is better inside season 1 (9 of 10 clubs), training is better from season 2 (9 of 10) because fatigue resets in the off-season and the growth stays. Neither is dominant over a two-season horizon.
- **Rotation is sometimes right:** the best-rested lineup beat the fixed lineup for 8 of 10 clubs (mean +0.12), not for the two clubs with a weak bench.
- **Train early, rest late** is the best simple schedule (+0.5 eff over the default in season 1, better for 10 of 10 clubs). The edge is about half a rating point, roughly 0.3 wins a season, so it rewards attention without making the decision trivial. It is a skill reward, not an exploit.
- **Hero practice loses to stat training on raw rating** in every club, as expected: familiarity is a weak lever by design (v1.5 re-tune) and the QA metric already gives each player his best hero. Its use is draft flexibility (learning a second hero), which this metric cannot see. It stays as a niche option; the Alpha test will tell whether it feels useless.
- Wins barely move (about 9 of 18 for every policy): training is a slow lever. The league as a whole grows about +0.8 Team Rating in season 1 and +1.3 by the end of season 2 (AI clubs train too), until players reach their ceilings. There is no youth intake yet, so a very long save will drift towards an ageing league; recruitment (Alpha 0.3) is the fix.

## Addendum: economy QA (Alpha 0.3, 2026-11-08)

Question (task D-007 tests): does money ever ruin a sensible club, and can recruiting snowball? Script: `tools/econ_qa.gd`; the managed club plays whole seasons with contracts, wages, sponsor and prizes live, and every other club stays as in the data. Policies: **passive** (never signs or releases, renews everybody, Steady sponsor), **active** (each pre-season and off-season swaps up to three of its weakest players for clearly better free agents when it can pay, renews only its best seven, Win Bonus sponsor), **active_steady** (same, Steady sponsor). Six seasons for passive, five for the others, all 10 clubs, one seed per club (so read the averages, not single seasons).

| Policy | Clubs dismissed | Lowest cash ever | Average rating vs league, season 1 to last | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| passive | 0 of 10 | 2,234 | weaker five clubs -4.0 (flat), stronger five +4.0 (flat) | Safe. Cash grows every season; strong clubs pile up 37,000 to 50,000 by season 6 through prize money (harmless: nothing expensive to buy, and the cap limits wages) |
| active_steady | 0 of 10 | 93 | all ten clubs together +3.0 in season 1 rising to +4.8 in season 5 (passive is 0.0 by construction) | Recruiting is a real lever: the rank of four of the six weakest clubs improved to 2nd to 4th within 3 to 5 seasons. Cash is tight in season 2 for the weakest clubs (as low as 93) but never negative |
| active (Win Bonus sponsor), first settings | 6 of 10 | -5,285 | weaker five -1.2 rising to +0.3, stronger five +4.2 to +4.3 | The recruiting gain is large for the weaker half (+2.8 to +4.4 over passive) and tiny for the stronger half (+0.2 to +0.3: the pool is weaker than their starters), so recruiting does not snowball. But the 12-win target is out of reach for weak clubs, so that gamble lost money for sure: too harsh |

Changes made from the first run (all in `game/data/economy_v0.json`):
1. **Sponsor targets scale with club strength.** Win target = the wins a club of that strength rank is expected to take (win rate from 72 percent for the strongest to 28 percent for the weakest, of 18 games). Rank target = one place above the club's strength rank, never better than 2nd or worse than 6th. Rewards re-priced so each gamble is roughly fair for the club it is offered to: Playoff Backer +5 R0, Win Bonus Brand +8 R0. A missed target costs 1.9 R0 (Playoff Backer) or 4.2 R0 (Win Bonus Brand) against Steady Partner, about 2 and 5 weeks of the starting wage bill. The Finance screen shows the season total with and without the target.
2. **Wage cap tightened from 1.15 R0 to 1.05 R0 a week**, so the cap is a real brake: room for about three upgrades at a weak club.
3. After these changes the Win Bonus policy still dismisses 4 of the 6 weakest clubs inside two to three seasons (that policy gambles the sponsor and spends to the cap at once). That is the intended price of an all-in strategy; the Club office and Finance screen warn from the first negative round, and the dismissal screen offers the last autosave. If testers find it unfair, widen `dismiss_rounds` (8 now) before touching prices.

Known gaps: only one seed per club and 5 to 6 seasons (the 10-season GDD target is not met yet, the long runs take about 40 minutes on this machine); AI clubs do not trade, so strong clubs have no cash sink. Both go to the next economy pass if testers report money feeling empty.



## Addendum: Bonds, Form and Morale in the loop (Alpha 0.4, 2026-11-08)

Question (GDD 9.1 "re-run with bonds in the loop", 9.2 tests 2 and 3): do chemistry, Form and Morale change how well strength predicts results, and can bonds be farmed? Script: `tools/chem_qa.gd`; every club in turn is the managed club (the human side plays on the AI's lineup rules), whole league splits (18 games, no playoffs) are played with the new model and with the old fixed-TeamMod model (`World.qa_static`), same seeds, 4 splits per club, 40 splits per model.

| Model | Spearman (team rating vs league points) | Win rate, 3 strongest clubs | Win rate, 3 weakest | Standard deviation of points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Old: fixed TeamMod 0.96 percent, no Form or Morale | 0.826 | 0.707 | 0.314 | 10.7 |
| New: Bonds, Form, Morale live | 0.826 | 0.724 | 0.294 | 11.5 |

Reading: strength stays as good a predictor as before. The new model stretches the table by about 7 percent (the strong win a little more, the weak a little less) because Form follows results: after a winning run a starter carries up to +4.5 Form (1.8 rating points). It is a margin lever, not a snowball. Chemistry itself barely separates clubs: after one split every computer-run squad sits at a bond level near 75, so the bonus ranges 1.6 to 2.5 percent across the league; the managed club's edge comes from keeping its five together and from signing carefully (a new starter at Bond 20 costs about 0.5 percent at Bond 60).

Checks that are unit tests (`game/tests/bonds_tests.gd`):
- Chemistry curve (9.2 test 3): Bond 60 and COM 60 gives 1.915 percent (target 1.7 to 2.0); new club 0.96 percent; Bond 100 with COM 60 gives 3.19 and with COM 100 gives 4.2; replacing one starter costs 0.51 percent; a starter with no shared language multiplies his 8 of 20 values by 0.65 (synthetic roster).
- Pace: a pair needs 27 games together to pass Bond 85 at the default rate (design target "about 30").
- Bond farming (9.2 test 2): playing every match day and adding a scrim every tick is 9 percent faster than playing alone over a split (18 ticks), 18 percent on the first tick, so the limit of 20 percent holds. This needed the scrim base lowered from +3 to +1.5 a week (see GDD 7.7 implementation notes). Exhibition does not exist yet, so its route is not tested.
- Form and Morale rules: ranges, decay, personality swings, playoff weight, the benched-starter rule, AI squads, save round trip and migration of a pre-0.4 save.

Known gaps: one split at a time (no multi-season run with bonds, so the long-run effect of carried-over bonds is untested), no joint Game Plan rerun with chemistry (open item since Alpha 0.2), the Python reference pipeline and golden vectors still use the fixed TeamMod (the GDScript model is the only one with bonds).
